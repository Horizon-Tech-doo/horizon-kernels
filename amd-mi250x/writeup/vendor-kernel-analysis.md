# What vendor kernels and TileLang really do on AMD MI250X (gfx90a)

This document explains the speed gaps between our TileLang kernels and Composable Kernel (CK) flash attention, hipBLASLt and vLLM's Triton MoE kernel. It separates what was read or measured from what was inferred, lists what we refuted, and ends with a ranked experiment plan. Experiments E1, E2, E3 and K1 have run, and on 6 Nov 2026 tracks N1, N3, N4 (with K2, N7), N5 (with N2, N6, C12), C1 (with C7, C9) and C2P ran further rounds, then S5 (scaling) and V1 (decode in a running vLLM); their results and their review corrections are applied here. On 9 Nov the kernel-max rounds (PREFILL-MAX, DECODE-MAX, MOE-MAX, GEMM-MAX, each with an adversarial review and a fix round) and JPORT (form J landed) moved every standing again; section 1 opens with them and each of sections 5.4 and 5.6 to 5.8 opens with a 9 Nov block. Where they conflict, the newest entry in the section's "current" block wins and older blocks stay as a record. New text labels [computed] and [inferred] separately. Section 1 has the current standing.

## How to read this document

**Labels.** Every claim carries one of two labels.
- **[observed]**: read in source or a binary, generated by a compile, measured on a GPU, or computed directly from such numbers (the arithmetic is shown).
- **[hypothesis]**: inferred. No measurement isolates it.

**Evidence limits that apply throughout.**
- Disassembly evidence is static. hipBLASLt's kernel names were captured at runtime for four shapes (E1) and 16384³ (N4). CK's runtime kernel is still inferred.
- Results marked MB come from one GPU job (23500993): one run per configuration, 40 iterations after 5 warm-up, no confidence intervals, compiled by hand from cached C patched with regular expressions, all variants in one translation unit. E1 repeated the main ones (section 5.4); rows it did not repeat stay one-run numbers, for example r12864 and rr12864 at 25.21 and 102.56 TF.
- MB's MoE variants use synthetic inputs and have no correctness check. E2 replaced them with identical real-sized inputs in both stacks; use E2 for MoE. E2's routing is still a uniform softmax top-8, not a model's.
- E1, E2, E3 and K1 use hand-compiled C or working copies of scripts. **As of 6 Nov:** fixes are landed in `moe_fused.py` (N1), `gqa_decode.py` (N3), `gqa_fa.py` (N5, C edits on generated source) and `gemm_prefetch.py` (N4, hand asm); `bench_gemm.py --best` runs E1's staged kernels through the JIT with a hook (C1). The C2P series (0001–0009 on upstream main 6e8f599e) makes the prefill forms expressible in TileLang source and emits the hand GEMM asm from a pass; no PR is opened. `scaling/ep_moe.py` runs `FusedMoE` from the landed file, in a non-landed call form (S5).
- Blocks per CU was read from hardware only in E1's traced kernels (floor or ceil of B/110 on the micro; 17 to 20 at B/110 = 18.6 for rr25632w2 at 8192³). Elsewhere it is inferred.
- **Cycle figures in sections 2 to 5.6 that assume 1.7 GHz are off.** E1 measured 1381 to 1395 MHz under all-CU MFMA load, 1219 to 1420 MHz in real GEMM kernels on one node, 1625 MHz in a nop loop. Prefill, MoE and hipBLASLt clocks were not read. Treat those cycle figures as relative, not absolute. **9 Nov:** amdsmi's `current_gfxclk` reports the 1650 MHz target, not a measured clock (1812 of 2550 PREFILL-MAX readings are exactly 1650.0), so every prefill "% at clock" figure built on it is unreliable; GEMM-MAX uses `average_gfxclk` of the matched GCD (a measured average whose package includes the twin GCD); DECODE-MAX and MOE-MAX use probe kernels run beside or after the timed kernel, so their clocks are inferred. Cross-job TF moves about 5%, so compare ratios within a job.
- MFMA issue is measured: 32.04 ticks for 16x16x16 and 64.08 for 32x32x8 (E1 traces), assuming `s_memtime` counts core cycles. 191.5 TF is the arithmetic peak at 1.7 GHz; observed sustained rate is 156.6 TF (section 5.5).
- The MoE shape (H 2048, I 512, E 256, top-8) was recalled at first and is now read from the Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B checkpoint config (N1, job 23508902). Rows marked "recalled" below happen to be right.

1. **146 TF is not a ceiling; in the MFMA micro the limit we hit is clock [observed, E1; micro only].** `mfma_only` follows ceil(B/110), so 146.2 TF is B = 1024 rounding up to 10 rounds. Exact multiples reach 155.3 to 156.6 TF, 156.57 TF sustained for 25 s on one node. MFMA issue is the nominal 32.04 ticks, and the clock under this load is 1381 to 1395 MHz: 191.5 × 1392/1700 = 156.8 TF. N7 does not extend this to real kernels: "Performance Level: manual" is a site setting with DPM active, and in GEMM hipBLASLt runs at a lower clock than ours and wins on efficiency. Power as the cause in the micro stays a hypothesis.
2. **GEMM's ranking changed once data and schedule were controlled [observed, N4].** E1's direct table on fixed-exponent data (0.974, 0.90, 1.058, 0.933, 0.774) understated compiled kernels. A hand-written schedule beats hipBLASLt on randn, and with B placed 128 bytes off in A − B it does so at the cap on two nodes; see section 5.4. It is not a compiler result.
3. **Occupancy and scalar staging are real [observed, E1], and occupancy now works through TileLang's JIT with no fork [observed, C1].** The attribute takes 256x128x32 to 106.37 TF at 8192³, +28.0% over b12864, and 94.23 TF at 16384³, +49.6%; through the JIT 100.98 to 103.33 and 90.51 to 95.01 TF. Staging adds +20.7% at 4096³ and +6.3% at the 17408 shape, −1.3% at 8192³, −7.0% at 16384³.
4. **The direct global-to-LDS load runs on our GPU [observed, K1], and it does not lead for GEMM.** With overlap on, staging beats it by 19 to 26% at 1 block/CU and ties it at 2. Without overlap, hand-written direct beats hand-written staging at 2 blocks/CU by 2.5 to 10.6%. The load's failure modes are silent.
5. **hipBLASLt at runtime uses MT256x128x64, MT128x128x64 and MT128x256x64, all MI32x32 [observed, E1, N4]; "sized for one block per CU" is inferred, not observed. No direct-to-LDS in the sampled kernels with those tile names.** All 1060 fp16 TN solutions in the large library disable it. At 16384³ it is `MT128x256x64 MI32x32 MIWT2_4 MO40`, group_seg 55296. Byte identity between runtime kernels and sampled listings was not checked.
6. **MoE leads on our own stack, through a tile, not F5 [observed, N1].** F5 as specified stays at 1.02 to 1.04x at T = 4096; the new down tile 64x64x128 gives the lead. On clang 22 the lead needs `-misched=gcn-max-ilp` and, above T = 1024, a Square gate/up partition. See section 5.7.
7. **Prefill: no single driver at the base tile [observed, E3]; joint loads and overlap at bN32 pass CK at d256 [observed, N5].** E3's load-stall result was at bN16 and did not bound this. See section 5.6.
8. **`regstage` and `regpref` never tested register staging.** Their staging arrays live in scratch memory (section 5.2).
9. **bf16 is not faster than fp16 at the same tile [observed, E1].** The 22% gap came from comparing different tiles and from occupancy.
10. **Decode leads in the lab, and its baseline was wrong twice [observed, N3; read from source, V1].** The second time is V1's: the lab raced Triton's 2D kernel, serving uses the 3D one, and in a running vLLM plugin over stock Triton is 0.966 to 1.009 (5.10). TRI3D then raced 3D: about 0.87 to 1.37 at Hkv 4, 0.86 to 1.30 at Hkv 2, so the lead at B up to the threshold is gone. DECODE-MAX (9 Nov) then took the 3D standing to medians 1.138 and 1.104 with losses in three cells per head count (section 5.8). The first: The 1.73x gap gave 1.12 to 1.32x. vLLM's default at head size 256 is a generic paged kernel, not Triton, and on the real model it runs on LBHNC at page 784 in BF16, not the page-16 frame first assumed. See section 5.8.

## 2. What CK flash attention does on gfx90a

**Which kernel.** [observed] The image ships flash_attn 2.8.3 with 2775 gfx90a code objects. The families are 576 forward `BlockFmhaPipelineQRKSVSAsync`, 1040 SplitKV, 495 SplitKVCombine and 96 AppendKV. No plain QRKSVS family exists. Our baseline's lengths are all multiples of 128, so the `nopad` kernel is dispatched (`fmha_fwd.py:247-250`); `pad` and `nopad` listings differ by 0 lines after normalizing symbols. Evidence limit: the binary is consistent with CK e8709c24 (setup.py pins it), but the wheel's provenance is not proven, and kernel selection at runtime is inferred.

| 128 | 128, 128, 32, 128, 32, 128 | 256 (4 waves) | 2 | 31744 B | 228, 0 AGPR | 0 |

Sources: `fmha_fwd.py:509,513`, mangled kernel names, `llvm-readelf --notes` on `d128_nopad.co` and `d256_nopad.co`, CK-QR:115-131. Occupancy computed: 512/403 gives 1 wave/SIMD; 512/228 gives 2. The blocks-per-CU column is the requested template value, and metadata agrees with it; actual placement is inferred. K and V are not held whole in LDS: only 32 of the head dimension (K) and 32 of the 128 keys (V) are resident at a time, while S is 128 keys wide per pass. That is how CK gets a large softmax tile from small LDS.

**K overlap is direct-to-LDS.** [observed] `buffer_load_dword vN, s[40:43], 0 offen lds` has 40 static occurrences at head 128 and 72 at head 256 (grep on CK-BIN listings; ELF flags gfx90a). The AMD Instinct MI200 ISA guide section 9.1.9 documents buffer load to LDS for sizes up to dword, with M0 as LDS offset, and says a memory-buffer-to-LDS load does not return a memory violation: an out-of-range address masks EXEC and raises no fault, so errors there are silent. (Quote and sections rechecked in K1; the guide itself is not redistributed here.) K1 ran this path: EXEC still reads all ones after an out-of-range load, TRAPSTS is unchanged, and M0 at or above 0x10000 wraps and overwrites low LDS, so errors can overwrite data too, not only lose it. `vmcnt(N)` semantics as CK uses them hold: all but the last N loads have landed. The loop per K chunk (CK-QR:368-395; d128_nopad.co.s:768-810, :875-902):
- issue the next chunk (8 dword loads per wave, 256 B per load; 4 × 8 × 256 B = 8192 B = 128 × 32 × 2);
- `s_waitcnt vmcnt(8)`, so the previous chunk has landed while the new one stays in flight;
- `s_barrier`, so all 4 waves have the previous chunk;
- run 16 MFMAs per wave on the previous chunk.

After the last chunk it uses `vmcnt(0)` plus a barrier. The first chunk of the next key tile is issued before the last V MFMA (CK-QR:725-741), so overlap crosses tile boundaries. Barriers carry no `vmcnt(0)` (config.hpp:209-210).

**V overlap is register staging.** [observed] V goes global to VGPRs (8 registers per wave at head 128, 16 at head 256, via 2 or 4 `buffer_load_dwordx4`), through `v_perm_b32`, then `ds_write2_b32` (CK-QR:398, :519-553, :674-712; d128_nopad.co.s:941-944, :1383, :1419). So the earlier idea that "CK overlaps through register staging" is half true: true for V, false for K.

**LDS ring.** [observed] A 3-buffer ring shared by K and V. Head 128 uses K 1,2,0,1 then V 0,1,2,0 (explicit specialization). Head 256 uses the generic Make(): K 0,1,2,0,1,2,0,1 then V 2,0,1,2; the K order matches the `m0` writes in d256_nopad.co.s:1009-1654, the V order is computed only (CK-POL:293-335). Stride 9216 B at head 128. The layout pads on purpose: K per-wave stride 272 B, with the comment "Optimize this for lds_read speed" (CK-POL:523-548, :624-646). No measurement shows the padding helps.

**Softmax.** [observed]
- Hardware exp2 with log2e folded into scale: `v_fma` + `v_exp_f32` (setup.py:378, CK-KERN:372, d128_nopad.co.s:1438-1451).
- Row max: a chain of `v_max3_f32` within lanes plus one `ds_bpermute_b32` per partner lane (CK-QR:505-514, :603-606; d128:1386-1420). Static `ds_bpermute` count is 2 at head 256.
- Mask: applied only on edge tiles, skipped by one `s_cbranch_vccnz` over 394 instructions (d128:983; CK-QR:480-502). Masked kernels dispatch blocks in reverse row order (CK-KERN:1021).
- Normalization: `1/l` once per row, then multiply (CK-QR:781-795).
- MFMA: `v_mfma_f32_32x32x8f16`, 256 instances at head 256 (d256_nopad.co.s).

**No GQA packing.** [observed] Each query head is its own block group; K and V are re-read per query head (CK-KERN:1142,1146,1419; `mha_fwd.cpp:210-218`).

**CK is itself far from peak.** [observed, computed] 92.4/191.5 = 48%; 96.6/191.5 = 50% (LOG:167, LOG:169). Async plus overlap does not imply near-peak.

**Open in CK.** LDS metadata exceeds 3 buffers: 31744 − 3×9216 = 4096 B at head 128, 63488 − 3×18432 = 8192 B at head 256. Q (QLoadOnce makes `GetSmemSizeQ` 0, CK-POL:33-36), dropout and epilogue terms do not explain it.

### 5.2 Why `regstage` and `regpref` are slow: scratch memory

- [observed] In both, the staging arrays stay in scratch: `half_t a_r[32]` / `A_r[32]`, ScratchSize 144 B/lane at bK 64, 32 `buffer_store_dword` and 32 `buffer_load_dword` in the K loop (ASM `regstage_128_64.s`, `regpref_128_64.s`). The new tile's loads finish before the scratch stores and the MFMAs, so no load overlaps math.
- [observed] `regstage` allocates with `T.alloc_local` and `regpref` with `T.alloc_fragment` (`gemm_prefetch.py:71-72,116-117`). Both become loop-carried `half_t` arrays in the generated C. A fix must cover both allocators.
- [observed] Replacing them with `uint4` scalars gives ScratchSize 0. K step becomes barrier, `vmcnt` waits with `ds_write`, issue next loads, barrier, MFMAs with no `vmcnt` wait (MB `kernels.s`). r12864 25.21 TF becomes rr12864 102.56 TF at the same Occupancy 2, so scratch rather than occupancy is the cause (MB).
- [observed, compile only] Swapping `*(uint4*)` for an ext-vector pointer also gives ScratchSize 0 and overlap, at bK 32 and 64 (AUDIT `G_regstage.s`, `f_460994032ab3.s`). Plain ext-vector has 16-byte natural alignment where the struct has 4, so `ext_vector_type(4),aligned(4)` is the safer form; it compiles identically (`a4_regstage.s`). The textual swap is not generally valid: it fails to compile on f86b93d6a0ea. Why LLVM promotes vector types but not the struct was not traced.
- [hypothesis] Why bK does not matter: bytes per flop are the same at every bK with 128 rows, and scratch adds a cost tied to those bytes. The simple "scratch is capped at about 1 TB/s" version does not hold. The standalone `scr8` is slowest at one block per CU (0.94, 0.76, 0.62 TB/s from dyn 0 to 65024), the opposite of `regstage`'s trend, and `regstage`'s implied rate spans 0.79 to 1.27 TB/s. The "3x bytes" cap (102.4/3 = 34 TF) is exceeded at 256x128 (48.3 vs 45.5 TF), suggesting scratch mostly hits L2. Why fewer blocks per CU speeds up `regstage` (r12832: 25.25, 27.74, 40.75 TF at dyn 16384, 32768, 65024) is unexplained.

- [observed] 143 VGPR + 128 AGPR, Total 272, Occupancy 1 (ASM `base_256_32.s`). At 256 threads that means 1 block per CU [inferred]. Doubling the LDS allocation changes nothing: 52.59 vs 52.53 TF at dyn 24576 and 65024 (MB).

- [observed, K1, E1] The attribute is not a free setting: `w3` lowered hand kernels (g0_64w3 68.94 vs g0_64 97.60 TF; g1_64w3 has ScratchSize 36). Choose it per tile.
- [observed] 256x128 `regstage` at 48 TF hid a real overlap gain under the scratch cost: r25632 48.19, rr25632 84.38 TF at Occupancy 1, a factor of 1.75.

### 5.4 Dense GEMM

**9 Nov, GEMM-MAX rounds 1 to 5 [observed; ours over hb through torch, throughput, same process, one GCD, unplaced, hand asm; `gemm_prefetch.py` 2a1dfb8f].** Standing in section 1. Mechanisms, in landing order: (1) full-fill edge tiles (`gemm_hand_rows`: M and N round up to the tile and the buffer resources end at the true sizes, so edge rows load zeros with no copy; hb's own names at the losing shapes showed fill tiles such as MT96x256 at 2816³), which flipped 2816³, 7168x3584x4096 and 1024x7680x2048; (2) range-checked small-M tiles, PGR2-style small-M schedules and partial LDS waits (`plw`) at 1024x7680x2048; (3) 8 waves on a range-checked 256x192 tile (+2.0 to +11.7% at 7 shapes); (4) an XOR swizzle of 16-byte LDS chunks (c ^ (r & 7)), which removes the row padding so a 4-wave 256x256 tile fits 64 KiB (204 VGPR + 256 AGPR, occupancy 1, no spills) with 33% fewer LDS bytes per MFMA than 128x64 per wave; (5) a contiguous output for 5632x3200x4096 (`cn`).
